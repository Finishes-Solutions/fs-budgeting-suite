# Research — the cost data behind the budgeting engine

Every default the v2 engine uses comes from a file in `data/research/` (schema:
`data/research/README.md`), compiled into `app/research-data.js`. The app does no live
research and calls no paid data API: what ships is what was researched, reviewed and
committed. Each value traces to its evidence — a source title, URL, publisher, access
date, short exact quote or table reference, and a confidence rating — or is labelled
**derived estimate — verify** with the method that produced it. The app shows that
trail on every line (Budget page → Sources & assumptions, or the source badge on a line).

## How it was researched

- **First pass (2026-10-24):** fifteen parallel research agents, one per project category
  (residential and commercial were split in two), plus one each for escalation/regional
  indices and financing, soft-cost/contingency/fee/adjustment norms, and two for the
  building blocks. Each wrote one JSON file per type or block and validated it with
  `tools/validate-research.js`. A shared web-search allowance ran out partway through
  every agent's run; after that they worked only from pages they could fetch directly,
  which is the main reason so many values are derived. **No source, URL, number or quote
  was invented** — where nothing solid was found the value is derived from comparables
  and says how.
- **Main session:** construction-duration norms from the Census Bureau's C30
  start-to-completion tables (`_common/schedule.json`), the Custom type, and the second
  pass below.
- **Second pass (Phase 7, 2026-10-24):** run every type and the 16 samples through the
  engine, compare against the researched ranges, and firm up the weakest data — see
  [Second pass](#second-pass-phase-7).

### Normalization (one method, applied by `tools/build-research-data.js`)

`normalized = value × series[2026] ÷ series[dollar year] ÷ regional factor(region)`

- **Escalation:** Turner Building Cost Index annual averages 2010–2026 (2026 = Q1–Q3
  average), a selling-price index; BLS PPIs and others kept as alternate series.
  Forecast 4.4% (2026) then 4.0%/yr 2027–2030 — the midpoint of JLL, Turner & Townsend and
  Construction Analytics outlooks for 2027–28; 2029–30 rests on the long-run average.
  The engine escalates from mid-2026 to each phase's construction midpoint.
- **Regional:** U.S. DoD Facilities Pricing Guide 2026 Area Cost Factors for all 50
  states + DC and 98 metros (national = 1.00), cross-checked against RLB Q3 2026 city
  ranges. Metro → state → national fallback.
- Percentages and ratios are never normalized. Where sources disagree, all are kept as
  evidence and the default is a reasoned midpoint with the range shown.

## Weakest areas — verify these first

**Whole types resting on build-ups, proxies or consumer guides** (low confidence):
glamping resort (tent structure now sourced, the rest a build-up), equestrian facility
(barn per stall unsourced; arena from a secondary source), vertical farm (proxied from
multi-level cannabis), co-living and off-campus student housing per bed, BTR attached
(one illustrative pro forma), modular housing (ratio to site-built), manufactured-home
community per pad (one Vermont budget + one feasibility model), cabin and container homes
(consumer guides), boat ramp per lane (bottom-up), renovation/rehab (one Florida Housing
cap), industrial outdoor storage, truck parking and boat/RV storage (bottom-up),
food hall, golf driving range and family entertainment center, auto dealership
(RSMeans 2019 + estimated sitework), brewery/taproom and winery tasting room.

**Specific numbers that drive many budgets:**

1. **Every CSI split except garden multifamily (RAND) and car wash is derived.** Totals
   are unaffected, but division-level lines are allocations, not published shares.
2. **Impact fees for multifamily and non-residential uses** rest on the Duncan
   Associates 2019 national survey of jurisdictions that *charge* fees, escalated with a
   construction-cost index — likely high as a national average. Single-family and
   water/sewer fees were recalibrated to NAHB 2024 in the second pass. Use the Impact
   Fees step (jurisdiction schedule) for real projects; Texas fees in particular are far
   lower than the national average (RAND: under $1,000/unit).
3. **Financing terms** (LTC 60%, fee 1%, spread over SOFR) are derived; only the market
   rates (SOFR, prime, Treasuries) are sourced.
4. **Hotel defaults** all depend on one judgment factor: HVS "building & site
   improvements" × 0.91 to strip permits/engineering and contingency.
5. **Medical office** $400/SF splits a 2× disagreement between RSMeans national data and
   Cumming metro ranges; ASC and urgent care are ratios off it.
6. **Size-adjustment exponent** (−0.08, Terner-derived), per-story premium (1%),
   Type IV mass-timber factor, third-party report and land due-diligence allowances,
   property tax during construction (1.1%/yr) — derived.
7. **Hawaii and Alaska regional factors** are adjusted judgments (DoD gives ~2.0–2.2);
   San Francisco, Las Vegas, Phoenix and Washington DC differ 15–35% between DoD and RLB.
8. **Building blocks with no evidence at all:** dredging, boat-ramp lane, steel pile,
   dock pedestal, tuck-under and carport parking, heavy site development, transformers,
   septic system, water well, masonry screen wall, underground detention, water-quality
   unit, interior demolition, soil remediation, UST removal, medical gas, lab HVAC,
   kitchen hood and commercial kitchen, grease interceptor, mass-timber shell, canopy,
   mail kiosk, wayfinding package.
9. **Older dollars** escalate a long way: childcare and house of worship (2019),
   auto dealership (2019), clubhouse and fitness room blocks (RSMeans 2019), emergency
   generator (2014). The Turner index carries them to 2026 (+40–50%).

### Residential

**Accessory dwelling unit (ADU)** (`adu`, per unit) — baseline $80,000 / $180,000 / $360,000; all-in range check $70,000–$550,000. Backyard or attached second unit on an existing single-family lot. Per-SF cost is high ($150–400/SF), because a full kitchen, bath and utility service are spread over only 400–900 SF and projects are one-offs with tight site access. Utility connections, especially new meters and long trenches, are the biggest swing items. The evidence is owner surveys from California and Portland, which report all-in cost including design and permits, plus 2026 contractor ranges. There is no national government benchmark, so confidence is medium-low.

**Affordable / LIHTC multifamily** (`affordable-lihtc`, per unit) — baseline $185,000 / $255,000 / $400,000; all-in range check $230,000–$750,000. Income-restricted new-construction rental financed with 9% or 4% LIHTC, usually garden or mid-rise. Hard costs per SF run above market-rate (smaller units, durability/green standards, accessibility, community space, sometimes prevailing wage) and soft costs are much heavier (syndication, legal, developer fee, reserves). Strong public data: Florida Housing's 2026 hard-cost-per-unit factors by building type, RAND's LIHTC vs market-rate cost decomposition for TX/CO/CA, and California TCAC-based TDC medians; national hard-cost averages are older.

**Assisted living / memory care** (`assisted-living-memory-care`, per bed) — baseline $195,000 / $255,000 / $380,000 *(derived estimate — verify)*; all-in range check $220,000–$560,000. Licensed assisted living with a secured memory-care wing: small private units (~350-550 SF), large dining/activity/care common areas, commercial kitchen, nurse call, wander management, generator and institutional-grade fire protection. Per-SF cost runs ~20% above independent living (Weitz 2025-26), and per bed it depends on gross SF per bed. Per-SF data are national survey ranges; per-bed figures are derived, with one older project as a check. Memory-care-specific cost data were not found publicly.

**Build-to-rent — attached/horizontal** (`btr-attached`, per unit) — baseline $150,000 / $228,000 / $335,000 *(derived estimate — verify)*; all-in range check $165,000–$450,000. Single-owner rental communities of attached townhomes, duplex/cottage clusters or one-story 'horizontal apartments', with private drives and an amenity center. Cost sits between for-sale townhomes and garden apartments: shared walls and compact 1–3 bedroom plans, but every unit has its own ground-floor entry, roof and often a yard. Per NAHB's 2016 profile, 77% of single-family built-for-rent homes were attached. The only cost line-items found are an illustrative 2026 Sun Belt BTR townhome pro forma and NAHB's community-level capitalization figure, so the default is derived from the townhomes file. Confidence is low-medium.

**Build-to-rent — detached** (`btr-detached`, per unit) — baseline $175,000 / $270,000 / $410,000 *(derived estimate — verify)*; all-in range check $190,000–$550,000. Purpose-built single-family rental community of detached homes on one parcel, held by one owner, with private streets and an amenity package. Homes are smaller and simpler than for-sale product (a 2016 NAHB/Census profile shows a 1,500 SF median, mostly one story, fewer garages). Because the whole community is on-site, the baseline hard cost includes the horizontal infrastructure (streets, utilities, stormwater). Public cost data is thin: NAHB's community-level capitalization figure and two 2026 trade-guide estimates. The default is derived from production single-family costs plus horizontal infrastructure. Confidence is low-medium.

**Co-living** (`co-living`, per bed) — baseline $65,000 / $100,000 / $165,000 *(derived estimate — verify)*; all-in range check $75,000–$240,000. Purpose-built co-living: private furnished bedrooms (often with private bath) clustered in suites around shared kitchens and lounges, leased by the room, usually urban podium or mid-rise. Structure costs follow podium multifamily; per-bed cost is driven by gross SF per bed (~300-350), bathroom density and furnishing. No public US per-bed construction budget was found in this pass, so all cost values are derived from the multifamily files and should be verified.

**Custom home** (`custom-home`, per SF) — baseline $188 / $272 / $470 *(derived estimate — verify)*; all-in range check $199–$1,045. One-off architect-designed detached home on the owner's lot, priced per finished SF. Cost is driven by design complexity, finishes, glazing, site conditions (slopes, hillside foundations) and region. Census contract-price data gives a solid national floor ($172 median, $198 average per SF, 2025), but it covers all contractor-built homes, including plan-book houses. Architect-designed work runs higher. The default mid is a derived premium over the Census average; the only architect-grade evidence is from high-cost Los Angeles fire rebuilds.

**Duplex / triplex / fourplex** (`duplex-fourplex`, per unit) — baseline $114,985 / $193,384 / $308,369 *(derived estimate — verify)*; all-in range check $125,438–$470,394. Small 2–4 unit 'missing middle' buildings, usually wood frame on a single lot. Per-SF cost runs a little above townhomes: small project scale, repeated kitchens and baths in smaller units, and fire separations. Triplexes and fourplexes often trigger commercial-code (IBC R-2) sprinklers and separate utility services. Direct cost data is thin. The only primary cost points are Washington permit valuations; national values are derived from townhome and single-family data. Treat as a derived estimate.

**Garden multifamily** (`garden-multifamily`, per unit) — baseline $140,000 / $195,000 / $290,000; all-in range check $150,000–$420,000. 2-4 story Type V wood-frame walk-ups on surface parking - the lowest-cost multifamily product; cost is driven by framing, MEP, finishes and a meaningful (~12-14%) on-site development share. Good data: an elemental breakdown from RAND (Trammell Crow Residential garden projects), RLB city ranges, RSMeans, a Florida HFA hard-cost factor and several developer/contractor ranges; per-unit market-rate data are thinner than per-SF data.

**High-rise multifamily** (`highrise-multifamily`, per unit) — baseline $320,000 / $470,000 / $800,000; all-in range check $360,000–$1,150,000. Type I concrete (or steel) residential towers, typically 12+ stories, with structured or below-grade parking. Concrete frame, window-wall/curtain-wall enclosure, high-speed elevators, pressurization and fire-pump systems and slow vertical schedules put it roughly 1.4x podium per SF. Evidence is moderate: RSMeans 8-24 story range, RLB city multifamily ranges, Terner (California regression) and Brookings Type-I premiums, Florida HFA high-rise factor and an illustrative developer range; no public elemental budget was found.

**Manufactured / mobile home community** (`manufactured-home-community`, per pad) — baseline $36,586 / $67,946 / $114,985 *(derived estimate — verify)*; all-in range check $41,813–$229,970. Land-lease community of HUD-code manufactured-home pads. The baseline cost is horizontal: private streets, water/sewer/storm, electric distribution, pads with hookups, landscaping and common amenities. The homes themselves are an optional line item. Per-pad cost is very sensitive to scale, because fixed infrastructure is spread over the pad count; the Anchorage study halves per-unit site cost going from 20 to 40 units. Cost also depends on whether public utilities are at the boundary. The only public per-pad budgets found are small, high-cost projects (Vermont, Alaska), so the national default is derived downward for scale and region. Home prices are solid (Census MHS).

**Modular / prefab housing** (`modular-prefab-housing`, per unit) — baseline $110,000 / $190,000 / $415,000 *(derived estimate — verify)*; all-in range check $110,000–$550,000. Factory-built volumetric modules or panelized building systems, built to state/IRC/IBC codes (not HUD-code; see manufactured-home-community), then shipped and craned onto site-built foundations. The factory package is ~55–65% of hard cost. Transport, crane/set, foundations, mate-line finishing and site utilities are the rest. Published claims are of up to 20% hard-cost savings and 40–50% schedule savings (Terner Center 2017), but in practice cost is often near parity with site-built. No national cost series exists, so the default is derived as a ratio to site-built residential. Confidence is low-medium.

**Podium multifamily (5-over-1/2)** (`podium-multifamily`, per unit) — baseline $225,000 / $305,000 / $460,000; all-in range check $250,000–$650,000. Four to five floors of Type III/V wood framing over a one- or two-level concrete podium holding parking, lobby, amenity and often retail. The concrete podium, elevators, Type III fire-retardant framing and urban sites put it ~1.5x garden cost per unit. Evidence: RLB city multifamily ranges, RSMeans 4-7 story, Florida HFA mid-rise factors, developer/contractor ranges and older architect ratios; no public elemental breakdown was found, so the CSI split is derived.

**Senior / independent living** (`senior-independent-living`, per unit) — baseline $250,000 / $350,000 / $530,000 *(derived estimate — verify)*; all-in range check $280,000–$780,000. Age-restricted, service-enriched rental (or entry-fee) apartments with central dining, commercial kitchen, wellness, activity and transportation spaces; usually 3-5 story wood or podium buildings, sometimes with cottages. Per-SF cost is close to podium multifamily but the large common-area share (~30-40%) raises gross SF per unit. Per-SF data come from Weitz's national senior-living cost survey (2025-26); per-unit figures are derived via gross SF per unit.

**Single-family home (production)** (`single-family`, per unit) — baseline $229,970 / $350,182 / $533,113; all-in range check $209,064–$783,990. Production/spec detached homes. Cost is driven by finished floor area, finish level and region; framing, MEP rough-ins and interior finishes are ~60% of construction cost. The data is strong: NAHB's biennial Cost of Constructing a Home survey gives a full dollar breakdown, and Census Survey of Construction gives national medians for size and price per SF. The two disagree. NAHB's builder sample is not representative and runs higher than the large-builder Census medians, so the default sits between them.

**Student housing** (`student-housing`, per bed) — baseline $80,000 / $115,000 / $175,000 *(derived estimate — verify)*; all-in range check $90,000–$260,000. Purpose-built off-campus student housing leased by the bed: 3-5 bedroom units with a bathroom per bedroom, furnished, built as garden, wrap, podium or high-rise near campus. Per-bed cost is driven by the building form (same structure economics as conventional multifamily), bathroom density and furnishing. Public per-bed budgets found are on-campus university projects (Utah 2022, UTEP 2025), which carry dining/student-life space; off-campus figures are therefore derived from the multifamily files and flagged for verification.

**Townhomes** (`townhomes`, per unit) — baseline $167,251 / $282,236 / $459,941; all-in range check $177,704–$627,192. Attached 2–3 story wood-frame townhouses, for sale or rent. Shared party walls save exterior envelope and lot area. Rated fire separations, more stairs and higher kitchen/bath density per SF offset part of that, so per-SF cost is close to detached homes. National data is indirect: Census sale prices for attached homes, translated with NAHB cost shares. The only direct cost points are permit valuations and a builder survey from Washington State. Confidence is medium.

**Wrap / mid-rise multifamily** (`wrap-midrise`, per unit) — baseline $200,000 / $265,000 / $360,000; all-in range check $220,000–$520,000. 4-5 story Type III/V wood-frame apartments wrapped around a free-standing precast or post-tensioned parking deck (the 'Texas donut'). Cost sits between garden and podium: the garage (~1.3 spaces/unit at ~$30k/space) adds ~15% to the per-unit hard cost, plus elevators and interior corridors. Product-specific data are thin - two developer/educational ranges, older architect ranges and broad RLB/RSMeans multifamily bands - so the mid leans on ratios to garden and podium.

### Specialty residential & lodging

**Cabin / short-term rental resort** (`cabin-str-resort`, per key) — baseline $101,753 / $203,506 / $395,706 *(derived estimate — verify)*; all-in range check $90,447–$565,295. Clusters of 400-900 SF furnished cabins operated as short-term rentals. Cost per key = the cabin (prefab/kit vs. site-built custom is the biggest swing, roughly $80-$500/SF per consumer cost guides) plus per-cabin site infrastructure (septic or sewer, water, power, driveway/parking, decks) and a share of shared amenities. No resort-scale budget was retrieved; the default is a derived build-up from consumer cost guides and the RV/campground infrastructure evidence. Confidence low.

**Campground** (`campground`, per site) — baseline $10,886 / $32,657 / $87,086 *(derived estimate — verify)*; all-in range check $8,709–$130,628. A tent / partial-hookup (water + electric) campground's per-site cost is dominated by the comfort station(s) spread over the sites, the loop roads and parking spurs, and whichever sites get water/electric. Public park projects (state and national parks) run far above private campgrounds because of accessibility upgrades, demolition, remote locations and prevailing wages. Evidence is public-sector bid/estimate data plus thin private data; the default is a derived build-up, confidence medium-low.

**Container home community** (`container-home-community`, per unit) — baseline $103,414 / $201,385 / $359,228 *(derived estimate — verify)*; all-in range check $87,086–$489,856. Homes built from converted shipping containers (typically 1-2 forty-foot high-cube boxes, 320-640 SF) set on piers or slabs with individual utility hookups, plus shared roads, parking and landscaping. The container shell is cheap but conversion (cutting, reinforcement, spray-foam insulation, windows, MEP) and code compliance bring $/SF close to small conventional homes. Only consumer cost-guide data was retrieved in this session; the community default is derived. Confidence low.

**Cottage court / pocket neighborhood** (`cottage-court`, per unit) — baseline $146,345 / $256,103 / $439,034 *(derived estimate — verify)*; all-in range check $156,798–$627,192. Small detached cottages (typically 500-1,100 SF, 1-1.5 stories) around a shared green. Cost behaves like small single-family construction - high $/SF because kitchens, baths and envelope don't shrink with floor area - plus shared courtyard, walks, parking and individual utility services. No published cottage-court construction budget was retrieved; the default is derived from Census single-family contract prices and NAHB cost-share data with a small-home premium. Confidence low-medium.

**Glamping resort** (`glamping-resort`, per unit) — baseline $35,000 / $75,000 / $200,000 *(derived estimate — verify)*; all-in range check $32,657–$326,571. Safari tents, domes or yurts on raised platforms with power (and often water/ensuite bath), linked by paths to a central lodge/bathhouse. Per-unit cost swings mainly on whether units have ensuite plumbing and HVAC versus a shared bathhouse, and on the structure type (bell tent vs. safari tent vs. geodesic dome). No sourced glamping development budget could be retrieved in this session (web search budget exhausted); every value is a derived build-up from campground/RV infrastructure evidence and small-structure cost data. Confidence low.

**RV park / resort** (`rv-park-resort`, per site) — baseline $19,594 / $45,720 / $103,414; all-in range check $19,594–$152,400. Cost per site is driven by wastewater (municipal sewer vs. septic/drainfield vs. package plant), electrical service (50A pedestals and distribution), road and pad surfacing (gravel vs. asphalt/concrete) and how much building (bathhouse, clubhouse, store, pool) is spread over the sites. Public-sector bid-grade evidence (Missouri, Utah state parks) runs $50k-$112k per site; private-developer itemizations run roughly $15k-$40k per site. The default sits between them, weighted toward private development; confidence is medium.

**Tiny home community** (`tiny-home-community`, per pad) — baseline $21,771 / $48,986 / $97,971 *(derived estimate — verify)*; all-in range check $21,771–$141,514. A tiny home community is priced per pad: utility stubs (water, sewer, 50-100A electric), a foundation or pier/parking pad, roads, landscaping and a share of a community building, with the home itself either owner-supplied (tiny house on wheels) or developer-supplied (optional line item). Published budgets are almost all supportive/transitional villages (Quixote Village WA, Los Angeles tiny home villages), so the market-rate default is derived from those and from the RV park per-site evidence. Confidence low-medium.

### Hospitality

**Boutique hotel** (`hotel-boutique`, per key) — baseline $188,158 / $271,783 / $439,034 *(derived estimate — verify)*; all-in range check $229,970–$679,458. Independent or lifestyle/soft-brand hotel (~50–200 keys), usually urban and often an adaptive reuse, with design-forward finishes and a destination restaurant/bar or rooftop. No survey reports boutique hotels as a category, so the default is derived between HVS select-service and full-service per-key costs and RLB metro 3–5 star $/SF; HVS "redevelopment projects" medians anchor the adaptive-reuse case. **Extended-stay hotel** (`hotel-extended-stay`, per key) — baseline $99,305 / $141,118 / $209,064; all-in range check $125,438–$303,143. All-suite extended-stay hotel (midscale, e.g., economy/midscale studio brands, through upscale, e.g., Residence Inn / Homewood class) with in-room kitchens and minimal public space. Larger rooms but low public-space ratio make cost per SF lower than select-service; midscale and upscale segments differ by ~60–70% per key, so the default blends them. **Full-service hotel** (`hotel-full-service`, per key) — baseline $219,517 / $303,143 / $439,034; all-in range check $313,596–$731,724. Upscale / upper-upscale full-service hotel (~200–500 keys, mid/high-rise concrete or steel) with restaurant and bar, ballroom and meeting space, and full back-of-house. Cost per key is driven by public-space ratio (meeting/F&B SF per key), structure and urban location; structured parking can add materially. Good national data from four HVS cost-survey editions, plus RLB 2026 metro $/SF and RSMeans national models.

**Motel conversion / renovation** (`motel-conversion`, per key) — baseline $17,980 / $53,941 / $101,888 *(derived estimate — verify)*; all-in range check $21,576–$155,829. Renovation of an existing 1–3 story exterior-corridor motel (rooms, baths, exterior, systems), repositioned as a refreshed/boutique motel or converted to interim or permanent housing. Cost per key spans ~10x with scope: light life-safety/ADA work for interim housing (~$16k–$19k/unit in California Homekey projects) up to full gut with kitchenettes and MEP replacement. Hard cost here excludes acquisition. Sources: Homekey case studies, the Nehmer/HVS Design Hotel Cost Estimating Guide (2021 renovation unit costs) and RSMeans new-motel cost as a ceiling; the mid is a build-up, so verify.

**Resort** (`resort`, per key) — baseline $365,862 / $574,926 / $1,149,852 *(derived estimate — verify)*; all-in range check $470,394–$2,090,640. Destination resort (upper-upscale to luxury) with multiple pools, spa, several F&B outlets, recreation and extensive landscaped grounds, typically low-rise on a large site. Per-key cost is driven by amenity area per key, villa/casita share and remote-site logistics, so the range is very wide. No survey reports resorts separately; the default is derived from HVS full-service and luxury per-key building costs (luxury includes many resorts) and RLB 5-star $/SF.

**Select-service hotel** (`hotel-select-service`, per key) — baseline $104,532 / $146,345 / $240,424; all-in range check $146,345–$334,502. Branded 4–5 story select/limited-service hotel (Hampton / Courtyard class) of ~80–160 keys with breakfast area, small meeting room, fitness and pool; cost is driven by guestroom count, framing system (wood vs. concrete/steel) and market. Data quality is good: four editions of the HVS U.S. Hotel Development Cost Survey (2022–2025 data) give national per-key medians by component, cross-checked with RLB Q2 2026 3-star $/SF and an HVS feasibility budget. HVS building costs include permits, engineering and hard contingency, so they are scaled to our hard-cost definition.

Eatertainment boxes (bowling, golf simulators, arcade, full kitchen and bars) of ~25,000–50,000 SF, often in converted big-box retail. Building cost tracks RSMeans bowling/theater models; the big swing items are games/lanes/simulators (FF&E) and the level of interior design. Public-company capital data (Dave & Buster's: ~$16M per new store, ≈$480/SF all-in) bracket the all-in cost; hard cost is derived from that and RSMeans.

**Event / wedding venue** (`event-venue`, per SF) — baseline $107 / $204 / $452 *(derived estimate — verify)*; all-in range check $102–$588. Banquet/wedding venue: one large clear-span hall with a catering (warming or full) kitchen, bridal suites, restrooms, outdoor ceremony lawn/terrace and parking. Building cost tracks RSMeans social-club and country-club models; finish level (rustic barn vs. estate) and grounds drive the spread. Texas TDLR filings for new banquet halls (2022–24) show owner-estimated costs of ~$70–110/SF (one $417/SF outlier), well below RSMeans-based figures (~$235/SF in 2023 $ with sitework); the mid splits the difference and should be verified.

**Farmers market / open-air pavilion** (`farmers-market-pavilion`, per SF) — baseline $54 / $103 / $218 *(derived estimate — verify)*; all-in range check $49–$283. Open-sided roofed pavilion (steel or heavy timber) over vendor stalls/drive aisles with lighting, power at columns, water, and usually a restroom building. Public bids give ≈ $57/SF of roof for a structure-only canopy (2015) and ≈ $540k for a ~20-stall street pavilion (2022, restrooms extra). The default includes a pro-rata restroom and site/utility allowance, so it sits above structure-only bids.

**Fitness center / gym** (`fitness-center`, per SF) — baseline $94 / $235 / $392 *(derived estimate — verify)*; all-in range check $105–$491. Health club with open fitness floor, group-exercise rooms and locker rooms, either ground-up or a converted retail box. Building $/SF is anchored on RSMeans gymnasium/athletic-club models (2019); size and investment anchors come from public company filings (Planet Fitness ~20,000 SF clubs, Life Time ~97,000 SF clubs, Xponential studios ~$554k for 1,500–2,500 SF). Equipment is carried as FF&E. Pools/aquatics are optional line items.

**Food hall** (`food-hall`, per SF) — baseline $191 / $408 / $708 *(derived estimate — verify)*; all-in range check $163–$980. Multi-vendor food halls are usually adaptive reuse of an existing building; cost is driven by kitchen MEP density (hoods, grease, gas, power per stall), how fully the landlord builds out stalls, and the condition of the host building. Evidence spans TI-only operator budgets (~$15–175/SF, low confidence) to full public-market conversions ($367–$536/SF in 2014–15 dollars). Mid is a reasoned midpoint; no recent cost-consultant food hall benchmark was found.

**Food truck park** (`food-truck-park`, per pad) — baseline $38,100 / $97,971 / $217,714 *(derived estimate — verify)*; all-in range check $32,657–$326,571. Cost is driven by fixed shared amenities (restroom building, shade pavilion, seating plaza, lighting, grease handling) spread over a small number of truck pads, so cost per pad falls steeply with pad count. Public data are thin: a few municipal budgets/bids (Moses Lake WA, Albert Lea MN, Tyrone GA, North Summit UT) and Texas TDLR project registrations give a wide $30k–$200k+/pad spread; the mid is a reasoned build-up and should be verified.

**Golf driving range** (`golf-driving-range`, per bay) — baseline $32,657 / $119,743 / $380,999 *(derived estimate — verify)*; all-in range check $32,657–$653,142. Ranges span from single-level covered tee lines (tens of thousands of dollars per bay) to multi-level climate-controlled entertainment venues with full kitchens (Drive Shack $25–40M for 72–96 bays; Topgolf $15–60M per venue). Per-bay cost is dominated by the hitting structure, outfield netting/poles and, for entertainment formats, the restaurant/bar building. Entertainment-format costs are sourced from SEC filings; traditional-range costs are derived.

**Indoor / outdoor sports complex** (`sports-complex`, per SF) — baseline $146 / $220 / $335 *(derived estimate — verify)*; all-in range check $157–$439. Youth/amateur sports campus: a clear-span fieldhouse (courts, indoor turf) priced per building SF, plus outdoor synthetic or grass fields with lighting carried as per-field line items. Fieldhouse $/SF is anchored on RSMeans gymnasium/athletic-club models (2019) escalated by reasoning; outdoor field costs are derived allowances cross-checked against one municipal complex ($27M for 60 acres, 2017). Turf-field and lighting unit costs still need sourced bid tabs.

**Pickleball / racquet club** (`pickleball-racquet-club`, per court) — baseline $156,798 / $418,128 / $836,256; all-in range check $146,345–$1,045,320. Indoor (or covered) pickleball club: clear-span building (often pre-engineered metal or a converted big box) with ~2,500–3,500 gross SF per court including lobby, pro shop, restrooms/lockers and a small F&B bar. Outdoor municipal court bids ($61k–$172k per court, amenities block) set the floor for court-only work; indoor cost is derived from RSMeans racquetball/gym $/SF × SF per court. Three 2024–25 indoor projects give $154k (TX, pickleball-only), $367k (FL municipal contract) and $800k (FL premium club) per court; RSMeans racquetball/gym models cross-check $/SF.

**Shipping container park** (`container-park`, per container) — baseline $94,079 / $188,158 / $365,862 *(derived estimate — verify)*; all-in range check $83,626–$470,394. Retail/F&B units built from converted 20/40-ft shipping containers around a shared plaza with restrooms, stage and landscaping. Two recent US projects give all-in anchors (Atlantic City ≈ $273k per container total investment; Rio Rancho $10M on 1.3 acres); the best-known example (Las Vegas) reports container construction ended up costing about the same as conventional building, so a build-up from RSMeans retail/restaurant $/SF cross-checks the per-container hard cost. Hard-cost share of the reported totals is assumed.

**Trampoline / family entertainment center** (`family-entertainment-center`, per SF) — baseline $73 / $167 / $287 *(derived estimate — verify)*; all-in range check $78–$355. Indoor FEC (trampoline courts, ninja/climbing, soft play, arcade, party rooms, café) in a high-bay box — most often a converted big box or warehouse, sometimes a new pre-engineered building. Building cost is low per SF (simple shell, open floor); the attraction package (trampolines, play structures, ropes courses, games) is a large separate FF&E/equipment item. Values derived from RSMeans warehouse/retail shells (2019) and size data from Main Event; no franchise Item 7 table was retrieved in this session.

### Industrial

**Boat / RV storage** (`boat-rv-storage`, per space) — baseline $13,000 / $28,000 / $45,000 *(derived estimate — verify)*; all-in range check $10,000–$60,000. Fenced, paved lots of 7–10 acres with a mix of open, canopy-covered (three- or four-sided) and fully enclosed 12–14 ft wide × 35–50 ft deep spaces; building coverage only 35–45% because of wide drive aisles. Cost per space is driven by the covered/enclosed mix and paving spec. Evidence is trade-press supplier pricing per SF of canopy/enclosure (Inside Self Storage); the per-space total is a derived build-up and should be verified.

**Cold storage** (`cold-storage`, per SF) — baseline $170 / $240 / $350 *(derived estimate — verify)*; all-in range check $175–$480. Refrigerated/freezer warehouses cost roughly 2–3× a dry warehouse: insulated metal panel (IMP) envelope, heated and insulated slab for freezer zones, an ammonia or CO2 refrigeration plant (25–35% of cost), insulated dock doors and much larger electrical service; many new builds are 40–50'+ clear for automated storage. Public evidence is thin — the available per-SF ranges are contractor/advisory articles — so the default is cross-checked bottom-up against the dry-warehouse baseline plus the specialty-MEP building blocks.

**Data center** (`data-center`, per kW) — baseline $9,000 / $12,000 / $17,000; all-in range check $9,500–$24,000. Colocation/hyperscale data centers are priced per kW of critical IT load; 70–85% of construction cost is electrical (switchgear, UPS, generators, distribution) and mechanical (cooling) infrastructure, so cost tracks IT capacity rather than floor area. Evidence is good: Turner & Townsend (US$13.28/W Chicago, 2026), JLL ($11.3M/MW shell-and-core, 2026) and Cushman & Wakefield ($17.6M/MW all-in, 2026) agree once scope differences (land, sitework, soft costs, contingency) are reconciled. Liquid-cooled AI halls carry a 7–10% premium; IT equipment is excluded.

**Flex / light industrial** (`flex-industrial`, per SF) — baseline $115 / $160 / $230 *(derived estimate — verify)*; all-in range check $120–$310. Small-bay flex (20k–100k SF, 18–24' clear, 2,500–10,000 SF bays) combines a light-industrial shell with 15–30% office/showroom finish, more storefront glazing, demising walls and a grade-level door per bay. Per-SF cost is well above bulk warehouse. No public benchmark isolates flex, so the default is built from Cushman & Wakefield's small-building shell figure plus a basic office finish allowance, cross-checked with RLB warehouse ranges — a derived estimate.

**Industrial outdoor storage (IOS)** (`industrial-outdoor-storage`, per acre) — baseline $100,000 / $175,000 / $375,000 *(derived estimate — verify)*; all-in range check $90,000–$500,000. IOS sites are low-coverage (<10–20%) yards for trucks, trailers, containers and equipment: the cost is almost entirely sitework — grading, stabilized aggregate or paved surface, stormwater, perimeter security fencing, lighting — plus a small office/shop. The surface spec (gravel vs. asphalt vs. concrete) swings cost 2–3×. No authoritative per-acre IOS benchmark was found; the default is a bottom-up derivation from the paving building block, checked against one low-confidence developer blog.

**Last-mile / infill industrial** (`last-mile-industrial`, per SF) — baseline $110 / $150 / $220; all-in range check $115–$300. Smaller (≈40k–250k SF) infill distribution and delivery-station buildings cost more per SF than bulk warehouses: fixed costs spread over less area, sites are often brownfield, and the ratio of paved van/trailer parking to building area is several times higher. No public source isolates "last-mile" product, so the default starts from Cushman & Wakefield's 2026 small-building model ($144 psf at 109,200 SF) and RLB metro ranges for the infill markets where this product concentrates.

**Manufacturing plant** (`manufacturing`, per SF) — baseline $130 / $185 / $300 *(derived estimate — verify)*; all-in range check $135–$420. Light-to-medium manufacturing buildings (single-story, 24–36' clear) cost more than warehouses because of heavier/reinforced slabs, higher electrical service and distribution, process utilities (compressed air, process water, exhaust), more HVAC, and larger office/support areas — MEP is 25–45% of cost. Process equipment is excluded. Public evidence is limited to an RSMeans 2019 model and contractor ranges, so the default is a derived midpoint; heavy, clean or crane-served plants need a separate estimate.

**Self-storage — climate controlled** (`self-storage-climate`, per NRSF) — baseline $80 / $130 / $200 *(derived estimate — verify)*; all-in range check $85–$280. Multi-story (typically 3–5 stories) interior-access climate-controlled storage on 1.5–4 acre infill sites. Cost drivers: structural system (steel/PEMB vs. concrete podium), number of stories and elevators, unit-mix partitions and roll-up doors, HVAC, and efficiency (rentable vs gross area ≈ 70–80%). Public evidence is a scatter of announced project costs (Inside Self Storage roundups) whose scope (construction vs total, gross vs rentable) is unclear, plus one vendor cost table; the default is a reasoned midpoint, medium-low confidence.

**Self-storage — drive-up** (`self-storage-drive-up`, per NRSF) — baseline $55 / $80 / $115 *(derived estimate — verify)*; all-in range check $58–$150. Single-story pre-engineered metal buildings with exterior roll-up doors, separated by 25–30 ft paved drive aisles, fenced and gated. Building cost per SF is low, but paving, drainage and fencing are a large share because site coverage is only ~35–45%. Evidence: one vendor cost table and two announced Texas projects; default is a derived midpoint with low-medium confidence.

**Truck terminal / truck parking** (`truck-terminal-parking`, per space) — baseline $14,000 / $22,000 / $35,000 *(derived estimate — verify)*; all-in range check $13,000–$50,000. Secured, paved tractor-trailer parking (and optionally a cross-dock terminal building) priced per truck space. Cost is mostly heavy-duty paving, grading and stormwater, plus fencing, lighting, gate/guard house and cameras; driver amenities, a cross-dock building or reefer plug-ins are add-ons. Public evidence is limited to FHWA benefit-cost case-study assumptions ($35k–$75k/space incl. land or amenities), so the baseline is a bottom-up derivation from the paving building block.

**Warehouse / distribution** (`warehouse-distribution`, per SF) — baseline $75 / $100 / $150; all-in range check $80–$230. Bulk tilt-wall or precast distribution (32–40' clear) is one of the cheapest building types per SF; cost is driven by building size (fixed costs spread over more SF), slab/structure spec, clear height, dock-door count and the size of the truck courts and trailer parking. Data quality is good: Cushman & Wakefield publishes an annual model-building guide (2026: $78–$144 psf by size) and RLB publishes metro ranges, but the two differ in scope and basis, so the default leans on C&W for big-box product and uses RLB for the spread.

### Agriculture & energy

**Battery energy storage (BESS)** (`battery-storage`, per MWh) — baseline $156,798 / $277,010 / $433,808; all-in range check $146,345–$522,660. Utility-scale lithium-ion (LFP) BESS priced per MWh of energy capacity, default 4-hour duration. The DC block (cells, racks, containers) is 50–65% of cost and has fallen sharply since 2023, so 2023-dollar engineering estimates (EIA/S&L ≈ $414k/MWh EPC) now sit at the top of the range while 2025 market ranges (Lazard LCOS v10 ≈ $140–365k/MWh installed) define the middle. Data quality: good, but prices are moving fast and are tariff-sensitive — verify against current quotes.

**Cannabis cultivation facility** (`cannabis-cultivation`, per SF) — baseline $225 / $350 / $500; all-in range check $175–$650. Indoor licensed cultivation priced per SF of gross building area. MEP dominates: HVAC/dehumidification and electrical service plus lighting are each about 20% of cost, and MEP overall ≈ 50–60%. Published all-in figures run $175–550/SF; watch the denominator (canopy is only 55–70% of gross area) and whether the figure is a warehouse conversion or ground-up. Sources are contractor/consultant statements in trade press (2017–2026), so confidence is medium.

**Commercial greenhouse** (`greenhouse`, per SF) — baseline $16 / $35 / $65 *(derived estimate — verify)*; all-in range check $11–$98. Gutter-connected poly/polycarbonate or glass production greenhouse with heating, cooling, ventilation, irrigation and controls, priced per SF of growing-house footprint. Cost spans an order of magnitude: single-bay poly Quonset houses run ~$9–10/SF, wholesale gutter-connected houses ~$20/SF installed, retail and high-tech glass houses with lighting $40–60+/SF. Public data are mostly manufacturer/extension anecdotes (no cost-consultant benchmarks found), so confidence is medium-low.

**Equestrian facility** (`equestrian-facility`, per stall) — baseline $35,000 / $70,000 / $160,000 *(derived estimate — verify)*; all-in range check $25,000–$250,000. Boarding/training facility with a stall barn, covered riding arena, outdoor arena, paddocks/fencing and site work, priced per stall. The barn (~300 SF per stall incl. aisles, tack, feed and wash) and the covered arena (a fixed ~20,000 SF clear-span structure spread over the stall count) are each roughly a third of cost, so per-stall cost falls as stall count rises. ALL VALUES ARE DERIVED: no fetchable authoritative source was found this session (cost-guide sites returned 403 and the web search budget was exhausted). Verify with a barn builder/arena quote.

**EV charging hub** (`ev-charging-hub`, per port) — baseline $130,628 / $190,500 / $304,799 *(derived estimate — verify)*; all-in range check $119,743–$435,428. DC fast-charging plaza (150–350 kW dispensers) priced per port, including charger hardware, make-ready (switchgear, transformer, conduit), paving and canopy. Charger hardware is ~45–60% of cost; installation cost per charger falls sharply with site size (ICCT: ~$45k for a single 50 kW unit vs ~$18k each at 6+ units), while utility make-ready is the main wildcard (PSEG-LI: $78k–116k per port). Default anchored on 2024 Colorado NEVI awards (~$183–193k total per port); canopy and paving adders are derived.

**Solar farm (utility / community)** (`solar-farm`, per MW) — baseline $979,712 / $1,142,998 / $1,632,854; all-in range check $925,284–$2,177,139. Ground-mount single-axis-tracking PV priced per MWdc. Cost is dominated by modules, trackers/racking and electrical BOS. Scale economies are strong: LBNL finds projects over 250 MWac cost about 60% less per watt than projects under 20 MWac, so community-scale (1–20 MWac) projects sit at the high end. Data quality is high: an itemized Sargent & Lundy/EIA estimate, the DOE/NREL bottom-up benchmark and LBNL empirical plant data all agree within about 15%.

**Vertical farm** (`vertical-farm`, per SF) — baseline $200 / $325 / $550 *(derived estimate — verify)*; all-in range check $175–$700. Indoor controlled-environment farm (leafy greens/herbs/berries) with multi-tier hydroponic racks under LED lighting, priced per SF of gross building area. Cost is driven by the number of grow tiers (lighting and racking scale with tiers), HVAC/dehumidification sized to remove the lighting heat load, and the level of automation. No direct public per-SF benchmark could be retrieved this session (web search budget exhausted); the default is DERIVED from indoor cannabis cultivation component costs and a trade-press quote for multi-level indoor grows — verify.

### Civic & institutional

**Childcare / daycare center** (`childcare-center`, per SF) — baseline $235 / $329 / $457 *(derived estimate — verify)*; all-in range check $269–$604. Licensed childcare centers are one-story, residential-scale (often wood-frame) buildings of 6k-15k SF with many small classrooms, high plumbing density (child-height toilets and sinks in or next to each room), a kitchen, and a fenced outdoor play yard of ~75 SF per child. Public cost data is thin: values come from the RSMeans 2019 day-care model and a 2019 Purdue University child care budget, so the hard cost is a DERIVED estimate in 2019 dollars (the build script escalates) - verify against a franchise FDD Item 7 or local bids.

**Church / house of worship** (`house-of-worship`, per SF) — baseline $215 / $322 / $504 *(derived estimate — verify)*; all-in range check $242–$671. Houses of worship combine a tall, long-span assembly room (sanctuary/worship center) with fellowship hall, classrooms/children's wing and offices; cost swings with structure (pre-engineered metal vs. masonry/steel truss), acoustics and worship AV/lighting. Public data is thin: the hard cost is DERIVED (in 2019 dollars, escalated by the build script) from the RSMeans 2019 church model and one 2019 Texas megachurch permit value - low confidence, verify against local church-builder bids. Rule of thumb for the seat alternate unit: ~18-22 SF per seat in the worship room and ~40-60 total building SF per sanctuary seat.

**Community / recreation center** (`community-center`, per SF) — baseline $392 / $549 / $784; all-in range check $444–$1,150. Municipal community/recreation centers combine a high-volume gymnasium (and often an elevated track) with multipurpose rooms, fitness, a catering kitchen and lockers; adding an indoor pool is the single biggest cost swing. Evidence is moderate: one hard GMP (Des Moines 2024, with pool, ~$595/SF), the Virginia state database (multipurpose $555, gym $693, 2026) and a large Colorado center (~$760/SF); a dry (no-pool) center is set at ~$525/SF.

**Fire station** (`fire-station`, per SF) — baseline $444 / $653 / $993; all-in range check $470–$1,463. Fire stations are essential-facility (Risk Category IV) buildings: tall long-span apparatus bays with heavy slabs and aprons, oversized bay doors, vehicle exhaust capture, decon/gear rooms, 24/7 living quarters and emergency generators, plus large paved site areas - so they price well above other civic buildings of similar size. Data is good: 2023-2026 public estimates and bid awards from TX, FL, MD, OH, CA and MA cluster at ~$420-$720/SF in low-cost states and ~$950-$1,000+/SF in CA/MA.

**K-12 charter school** (`charter-school`, per SF) — baseline $272 / $376 / $523 *(derived estimate — verify)*; all-in range check $293–$732. Charter schools are typically 35k-90k SF one- or two-story buildings with standard classrooms, a combined gym/cafetorium, a serving kitchen and limited athletic facilities, often built design-build by charter networks or developers with leaner specs than district schools. No charter-specific cost dataset could be retrieved in this session, so the hard cost is DERIVED from state K-12 contract data (NC DPI 2024-25, ~$400-$480/SF incl. site) discounted for charter program and spec; verify against charter bond offering documents or network build costs.

**Public library** (`library`, per SF) — baseline $418 / $601 / $836; all-in range check $470–$1,254. Branch libraries are mid-size (10k-35k SF) one- or two-story assembly buildings with long-span reading rooms, lots of glazing and daylighting, heavy floor loads for stacks, meeting rooms and makerspaces; cost is driven by envelope quality, HVAC and site. Data is good: recent Texas state registrations, a Georgia owner estimate, the Virginia state cost database and a Library Journal-based median all fall in ~$500-$680/SF (2024-26), with older Midwest/Mountain planning rates near $320-$360/SF.

### Marine, parking & other

**Adaptive reuse** (`adaptive-reuse`, per SF) — baseline $157 / $261 / $418 *(derived estimate — verify)*; all-in range check $178–$627. Conversion cost (typically office-to-residential) is driven by floor-plate depth (light/air, need for light wells), plumbing riser density for new kitchens/baths, window and facade replacement, hazardous-material abatement, code upgrades (sprinklers, egress, seismic) and historic constraints. Public hard-cost data is thin: a 2025 NYC practitioner range ($300-375/SF hard) and Chicago LaSalle-corridor public budgets (total development cost $372k-$625k/unit, ~$387/SF for the Field Building). The national default is a derived regional normalization of those — medium-low confidence.

**Boat ramp** (`boat-ramp`, per lane) — baseline $200,000 / $550,000 / $1,200,000 *(derived estimate — verify)*; all-in range check $175,000–$1,700,000. A launch lane's all-in cost is dominated by three pieces: the in-water concrete ramp (cofferdam/precast planks, 12-16 ft wide), the courtesy/boarding dock, and the trailer parking it needs (~30-35 car-trailer stalls per lane per Virginia DWR guidance). No public per-lane cost benchmark was found; the default is a bottom-up derived estimate bracketed by a recent single-lane state project (RI, $177k reconstruction) and 2026 Florida FBIP grant requests of ~$0.75-1.24M for ramp replacements. Low confidence — verify.

**Custom (built from blocks)** (`custom`, per LS) — baseline $0 / $0 / $0 *(derived estimate — verify)*. A Custom component has no baseline price of its own: its hard cost is the sum of the building blocks the user adds (each priced from data/research/_blocks with its own sources) plus any hand-priced items. Soft costs, contingencies, fees and financing use the shared norms in data/research/_common.

**Interior finish-out / TI (general)** (`interior-finish-out`, per SF) — baseline $75 / $147 / $400; all-in range check $60–$600. Tenant-improvement cost for a commercial shell is driven by use (retail soft goods < office < restaurant), MEP density, and first- vs. second-generation space (2nd gen runs 10-15% less per Cushman & Wakefield). Office fit-out is well benchmarked by Cushman & Wakefield's 2026 Americas Office Fit Out Cost Guide (US average $162/SF incl. a 10% contingency) with a published trade breakdown; retail and restaurant figures come from secondary sources (low confidence). Default = general commercial/office-grade TI.

**Marina** (`marina`, per slip) — baseline $27,000 / $60,000 / $150,000; all-in range check $25,000–$250,000. Per-slip cost is driven by slip length/vessel class, dock system (timber vs. concrete floats, steel vs. concrete piles), utilities (power, water, fire suppression, pump-out) and exposure (wave attenuation). Site-specific heavy items — dredging, breakwaters, bulkheads, fuel systems — vary by orders of magnitude and are carried as separate line items. Evidence is a set of recent public project budgets (USFWS Boating Infrastructure Grant FY2025-26 summaries, Treasure Island, Berkeley); these are total project costs for transient facilities, so the default is medium confidence.

**Mixed-use (combined components)** (`mixed-use`, per SF) — baseline $225 / $295 / $450 *(derived estimate — verify)*; all-in range check $250–$650. Quick-mode fallback for vertical mixed-use (residential over ground-floor retail with podium/structured parking). There is no published 'mixed-use' $/SF benchmark; the blended default is derived from component costs (RLB Q3 2026 multifamily and retail ranges, the podium-multifamily file in this database) weighted ~85% residential / 15% retail, plus a small mixed-occupancy premium. For detailed budgets the engine should build mixed-use from its component types instead.

**Parking garage** (`parking-garage`, per space) — baseline $27,800 / $33,300 / $52,000; all-in range check $30,000–$80,000. Freestanding above-grade structured parking is well benchmarked: WGI's annual Parking Structure Cost Outlook (national median of hundreds of completed garages) is the anchor, with RLB-based city averages (UCLA ITS 2026) and a recent public design-build contract showing the upper range. Cost is driven by efficiency (SF per space), structural system, facade, sprinklers/EV mandates and market; below-grade parking is excluded and roughly 1.4x+ more expensive.

**Renovation / rehab** (`renovation-rehab`, per SF) — baseline $35 / $110 / $200 *(derived estimate — verify)*; all-in range check $35–$300. Same-use renovation spans a very wide range — cosmetic value-add (paint, flooring, fixtures) at a few tens of dollars per SF up to gut rehab of systems and envelope approaching new-construction cost. The best public anchor is Florida Housing's 2026 TDC test, which sets rehab hard-cost factors at roughly half of new-construction factors ($122k garden / $147k non-garden rehab vs. $256k / $284k new per unit). The default is a moderate-to-substantial rehab derived from that ratio and the new-construction files in this database — low-medium confidence; scope definition matters more than any benchmark.

**Surface parking lot** (`parking-lot`, per space) — baseline $4,181 / $6,795 / $16,202 *(derived estimate — verify)*; all-in range check $4,181–$22,997. Surface lot cost per space is driven by paved area per space (250-350 SF incl. aisles), pavement section (asphalt vs. concrete), storm drainage/detention, lighting and code landscaping. Public data is thin: one 2024 municipal engineer's estimate (~$5.3k/space), a VTPI calculator default ($5k suburban / $10k urban, 2022) and a 2025-26 university lot with full campus standards (~$15.5k/space). Default is a reasoned midpoint — medium-low confidence.

## Updating the research

1. Edit or add a file under `data/research/` following `data/research/README.md`. Every
   money value needs `dollarYear` and `region`; every value needs evidence or
   `derived: true` with a `method`. Never cite what you did not read.
2. `node tools/validate-research.js` — fix every error.
3. `node tools/build-research-data.js` — rebuilds `app/research-data.js` (the test suite
   fails if it is stale).
4. `node --test test/research-data.test.js` — every type must still price inside its
   range check and tie to the budget arithmetic.
5. `node tools/research-coverage.js` — refreshes the tables in this file.
6. `node tools/run-samples.js` — refreshes `docs/sample-output/`.
7. Commit all of it together. Saved budgets keep their numbers until the user edits them
   or chooses **Update to current research** (the banner names both data versions).
